# Grounding a plan in the manufacturer's manual — ePX3030, 9 Sep 2026

The first real job StepSpotter was built for is a **Westinghouse ePX3030 pressure
washer**, bought and still in its box. There is no assembly video for that exact
model. The official manual PDF does exist, and its assembly section is pages 10-12,
with nozzles on page 14.

Before this change the Planner saw one photo and its own memory of pressure washers.
After it, `research.py` finds the manufacturer's own manual, pulls the assembly pages
out of the PDF with page markers, and the Planner writes steps that follow the
manual's order and part names and cite the page each step came from.

Both runs below are live: real DuckDuckGo, real PDF, real Bedrock
(`global.anthropic.claude-sonnet-4-6`, us-east-1).

> **The photo is a stand-in.** The owner's own photos of the boxed unit have not
> arrived yet, so both plans were run against **page 9 of the manual itself** — the
> FIG.3 components diagram, rendered at 120 dpi. `data/` is not committed, so a copy
> of the exact image both runs saw is kept beside this file at
> `docs/research-epx3030-2026-09-09/standin-epx3030-fig3.png`. It is a **diagram, not a photo of the actual
> parts on the floor**. Every claim below about step ORDER and PART NAMES holds;
> nothing here shows how the Planner behaves on a real cluttered photo, and the
> re-run on the owner's photos is still owed.

Read that as: the manufacturer's own CDN was ranked above the ManualsLib copy, the
PDF was downloaded and cached under `data/manuals/`, and the pages picked out of a
53-page trilingual manual were **10, 11, 12, 13, 14** — assembly, then operation
(starting/stopping, installing nozzles). Pages 19-53 are the Spanish and French
copies of the same thing; they were not selected.

One thing that had to be fixed to get there: page 7 is a **safety-label** page whose
text contains the phrase "when starting and stopping the pressure washer". A plain
substring match pulled that page in and pushed page 12 out of the character budget.
Selection now prefers a marker that appears as a **heading line**, and only falls
back to a substring match when no page has one.

## 2. The plan, grounded (research ON)

```
job_id: job-20260909-195255-6226
job:    Assemble Westinghouse ePX3030 pressure washer  [diy_ok]
tools:  Phillips-head screwdriver, Your hands
manual: https://cdn.westinghouseoutdoorpower.com/owners_manuals/ePX3030_manual_web.pdf (pages 10, 11, 12, 13, 14)
video:  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5mOr9PLU3Hc
video:  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7hJjRkJHWA4
video:  https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLQGBi0cSI9NfiOzhnX4pZs7KLU5Ek6OPy

  1. Unbox and check all parts
     Carefully remove everything from the carton and lay the parts out on a flat surface. Check that you have: 1 spray gun, 1 lance (the long wand), 2 nozzles, 1 high-pressure hose, 1 low-pressure soap applicator, 1 handle, 3 mounts (the bracket clips), 4 screws, and 2 wheels.
     do not touch: power cord — do NOT connect to any outlet until all assembly is complete
     stop if: If any part looks cracked, bent, or damaged, stop and contact Westinghouse customer service at service@wpowereq.com or 1-855-944-3571 before continuing.
     photo must show: Photo showing all listed parts laid out and visible — no empty spaces in the lineup.
     source: manual p.10
  2. Slide mounts onto the frame
     Take one mount (a bracket clip) and slide its latch onto the metal frame of the pressure washer body. Repeat with the second mount on the other side of the frame.
     do not touch: power cord · on/off button
     photo must show: Photo showing both mounts sitting flush against the frame on each side.
     source: manual p.10
  3. Slide handle onto the frame
     Take the handle (the U-shaped bar used to push the unit) and slide it down onto the top of the frame of the pressure washer.
     do not touch: power cord · on/off button · mounts already placed
     photo must show: Photo showing the handle seated on the frame with both ends visible in position.
     source: manual p.10
  4. Screw mounts and handle to frame
     Using a Phillips-head screwdriver, drive the 4 screws through the mounts and handle into the frame — 2 screws secure the mounts, and 2 screws secure the handle. Do not overtighten; stop when each screw sits flush (level with the surface and no longer turning).
     do not touch: power cord · on/off button
     photo must show: Photo showing all 4 screws flush with the brackets/handle with no screw heads sticking up.
     source: manual p.10
  5. Attach both wheels to the frame
     Push one wheel onto its axle stub on the lower side of the frame; press firmly until you hear and feel a click, which means it is locked in place. Repeat for the second wheel on the other side.
     do not touch: power cord · on/off button
     stop if: If a wheel does not click after firm pressure, do not force it — check the axle for damage before continuing.
     photo must show: Photo showing both wheels attached flat against the frame with no visible gap between wheel and frame hub.
     source: manual p.11
  6. Connect lance to spray gun
     Push the lance (the long straight wand, item 10 in the diagram) into the front of the spray gun (item 9) and turn it clockwise (like tightening a jar lid) until snug — do not overtighten.
     do not touch: power cord · high pressure hose
     photo must show: Photo showing the lance inserted into the spray gun with no visible gap at the joint.
     source: manual p.11
  7. Pull-test the lance connection
     Grip the lance near the gun with one hand and the gun body with the other, then gently pull them apart to confirm the lance does not come loose.
     do not touch: power cord
     photo must show: Photo showing the lance still firmly seated in the gun after the pull-test.
     source: manual p.11
  8. Connect high-pressure hose to spray gun
     Pull back the collar (the sliding ring, item 3 on FIG. 8) on one end of the high-pressure hose, push that hose fitting firmly into the back of the spray gun, then screw the collar onto the gun's threads — tighten securely by hand but be careful not to cross-thread (force it at an angle), which would damage the threads and cause leaks.
     do not touch: power cord · lance
     stop if: If the collar feels like it is grinding or won't thread smoothly, stop — the threads may be cross-threaded. Unscrew and realign before continuing.
     photo must show: Photo showing the collar screwed fully onto the spray gun with no gap between collar and gun body.
     source: manual p.12
  9. Connect high-pressure hose to pressure washer
     Uncoil and straighten the other end of the high-pressure hose to remove any kinks, then pull back the collar on that end and push the hose fitting firmly into the threaded outlet (item 4, the round connector on the lower body of the pressure washer). Screw the collar onto the outlet and tighten securely by hand.
     do not touch: power cord · on/off button
     stop if: If the collar grinds or won't thread smoothly, stop — unscrew and realign to avoid thread damage.
     photo must show: Photo showing the collar fully threaded onto the washer outlet with no visible gap.
     source: manual p.12
  10. Pull-test both hose connections
     Grip the hose near the spray gun connection and pull firmly — it should not move. Then grip the hose near the pressure washer outlet and pull firmly — it should not move either.
     do not touch: power cord
     photo must show: Photo showing both hose ends still fully seated after the pull-test, with collars flush against their fittings.
     source: manual p.12
  11. Final visual check before first use
     Stand back and look over the whole assembled unit: both wheels are clicked on, the handle and mounts are screwed down, the lance is in the gun, and the hose connects gun to washer. Confirm the power cord is still unplugged — do not plug it in until you have connected a garden hose to the water inlet and turned the water on first.
     do not touch: power cord — do NOT plug in yet
     stop if: If any part looks loose, crooked, or unattached, go back to the step for that part before proceeding. Do NOT connect power until water supply is connected and running.
     photo must show: Photo showing the fully assembled pressure washer standing upright on its wheels with the power cord visibly unplugged and hanging free.
     source: manual p.10, p.13

```

**Does it follow the manual's order?** Yes, for everything the manual covers as
assembly:

| Manual order (pp.10-12) | Plan |
|---|---|
| unpack, check the INCLUDED LIST | step 1 |
| mounts onto the frame, handle, 4 screws | steps 2, 3, 4 |
| wheels | step 5 |
| lance into the spray gun | steps 6, 7 (7 is a pull-test) |
| high-pressure hose to the gun | step 8 |
| high-pressure hose to the unit | steps 9, 10 (10 is a pull-test) |
| garden hose to the inlet | **not its own step** — named inside step 11 |
| nozzles (p.14) | **not a step** — assembly ends before first use |

So: handle+mounts -> wheels -> lance -> hose to gun -> hose to unit, exactly in the
manual's sequence, with the manual's own part names ("mount", "lance", "collar",
"threaded outlet"). The garden hose and the nozzles were folded into the closing
check rather than given steps of their own; the job was worded as "assemble out of
the box", and the manual puts both under Operation, not Assembly. Worth watching, not
a defect.

**Page sources: 11 of 11 steps carry one** (p.10 x4, p.11 x3, p.12 x3, and step 11
cites "manual p.10, p.13"). The manual and the three video links are on the plan as
`sources`, filled in from the research object — the model is never asked to reproduce
a URL.

## 4. Before and after, in one paragraph

Without the manual the plan is confident, readable, and **misses the assembly**.
It never mentions the handle, the two mounts or the four screws — the only part of
this job that actually needs a screwdriver — and duly reports "no tools required",
where the grounded run asks for a Phillips-head screwdriver because the manual lists
four screws. It goes straight from unboxing to hoses, invents a wheel step that is
conditional ("if not pre-installed"), and adds a first-start test with the power cord
plugged in, which the manual's assembly section deliberately does not reach. It also
carries no manual link and no videos. The grounded run has 11 steps in the manual's
own order with the manual's part names, two explicit pull-tests, and a page citation
on every step that a person can check against the paper in the box.

One honest catch in the ungrounded run: every step still claims `source: manual p.9
FIG.3`. There was no manual in that run — the model read "English | 9" and "FIG. 3"
off the stand-in image, which happens to be a manual page. On a real photo of parts
on a floor there would be nothing to read, but it is a reminder that `source` is only
trustworthy when the research object actually put an excerpt in front of the model.
